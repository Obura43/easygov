Agriculture Projects

National agriculture development initiatives driving Kenya's food security and economic growth.

Agriculture Projects Overview

The Kenyan government has prioritized agricultural transformation as a key pillar for economic growth, food security, and employment creation. With investments exceeding KSh 15 billion in the last fiscal year alone, these projects target smallholder farmers, improve irrigation infrastructure, and enhance value chains across the country.

Key Projects and Impact

  • National Irrigation Expansion Program: Over 50,000 hectares of land irrigated in counties such as Kisumu, Nakuru, and Meru, boosting yields of maize, horticulture, and sugarcane by up to 30%.
  • Farm Input Subsidy Scheme: Distributed certified seeds and fertilizers worth KSh 3 billion to over 250,000 smallholder farmers nationwide, enhancing crop productivity and resilience.
  • Agricultural Mechanization and Extension Services: Procurement and deployment of 1,200 tractors and 500 combine harvesters to counties including Embu, Kitui, and Laikipia, reducing post-harvest losses by 15%.
  • Value Chain Development Initiatives: Support for agro-processing facilities in Nairobi, Eldoret, and Mombasa with loans and grants exceeding KSh 1.8 billion, creating over 10,000 jobs.
  • Climate Smart Agriculture Adoption: Training and equipping farmers in Turkana, Marsabit, and Baringo with drought-resistant crop varieties and water harvesting technologies, improving food security in arid regions.

Expected Outcomes

  • Increase national food production by 25% over the next 5 years.
  • Create 500,000 direct and indirect jobs in rural areas.
  • Reduce dependency on rain-fed agriculture and mitigate climate risks.
  • Boost Kenya’s agricultural export earnings by 15% annually.
