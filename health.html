Health Projects

Driving healthcare access and quality through strategic national investments

Health Projects Overview

The Kenyan government has prioritized health sector development by investing over KSh 80 billion since 2020 in infrastructure, equipment, and human resources across counties. These initiatives aim to improve healthcare access for over 50 million Kenyans by expanding hospital capacity, improving maternal and child health services, and controlling endemic diseases.

Major Health Projects

  • Expansion of Referral Hospitals: Upgrading and equipping 12 county referral hospitals including Moi Teaching & Referral Hospital (Eldoret), Kenyatta National Hospital (Nairobi), and Coast General Hospital (Mombasa).
  • Maternal Health Improvement: Constructed and operationalized 200 new maternity wings across rural counties such as Kisumu, Turkana, and Kitui, reducing maternal mortality by 20% since 2021.
  • COVID-19 Response & Vaccination: Administered over 30 million vaccine doses nationwide; constructed 15 isolation and treatment centers.
  • Disease Control Initiatives: Expanded malaria nets distribution to over 5 million households in endemic regions like Western Kenya and enhanced HIV/AIDS treatment access to 1.6 million patients.
  • Digital Health Records: Rolled out electronic medical record systems in 150 health facilities to improve patient care and data management.

Impact

  • Improved healthcare access for over 50 million Kenyans, especially in marginalized and rural areas
  • Reduced maternal and infant mortality rates by approximately 15-20% over 4 years
  • Increased hospital bed capacity by 25% in key counties
  • Over 1,200 new health workers recruited, including nurses and clinical officers
  • Strengthened disease surveillance and response systems, particularly for malaria, HIV/AIDS, and COVID-19
  • Enhanced healthcare infrastructure supporting universal health coverage goals
