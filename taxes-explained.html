Understanding Kenya's Tax System

A clear, accurate and simplified guide to how taxes work in Kenya

1. Personal Income Tax (PAYE)

PAYE (Pay As You Earn) is the tax deducted monthly from employees’ salaries. It is progressive — meaning higher income attracts higher tax. PAYE is one of the largest contributors to government revenue.

Monthly Taxable Income (KES) Tax Rate
0 – 24,00010%
24,001 – 32,33325%
32,334 – 500,00030%
500,001 – 800,00032.5%
Above 800,00035%

*All employees also enjoy a monthly Personal Relief of Ksh 2,400 which reduces PAYE payable.*

2. Value Added Tax (VAT)

VAT is charged on the supply of goods and services at a standard rate of 16%. Some essential goods (e.g., unprocessed foods, medicines, exports) are zero-rated or exempt to reduce the cost burden on households.

3. Other Major Taxes in Kenya

  • Excise Duty: Applied on goods such as fuel, alcohol, tobacco, motor vehicles and some financial services.
  • Rental Income Tax: - Residential landlords: 10% on gross rent (monthly or quarterly). - Commercial property owners: Taxed under normal income tax rules.
  • Corporate Tax: - Resident companies: 30% - Non-resident companies: 37.5%
  • Turnover Tax (TOT): For businesses making Ksh 1 million – 25 million annually, charged at 3% of gross sales. (Filing is monthly on iTax.)
  • Capital Gains Tax (CGT): Charged at 15% on profit from the sale of land, buildings, or investments.
  • Digital Service Tax (DST): Charged at 1.5% of the value of digital marketplace services offered by non-residents.
  • Withholding Tax (WHT): Deducted from payments like professional fees, interest, dividends, royalties, contractor payments, etc. Rates range from 5% to 20%.
  • Import Duty: Applied on goods entering Kenya, based on customs valuation (CIF price).

4. Tax Reliefs & Deductions

These reduce the final tax you pay:

  • Personal Relief: Ksh 2,400 per month
  • Insurance Relief: 15% of premiums (max Ksh 5,000/month)
  • Pension Contributions Relief: Up to Ksh 20,000 per month
  • Affordable Housing Relief: 15% of housing savings
  • Mortgage Interest Relief: Up to Ksh 25,000 per month
  • Disability Exemption: First Ksh 150,000 per month tax-free (for registered persons with disability)

Important Filing Information

All Kenyans with a KRA PIN must file taxes annually by June 30th, whether employed, self-employed, or unemployed.

Filing is done online through the KRA iTax Portal. Penalties apply for late filing or late payment.
