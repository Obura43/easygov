National Budget 2024/2025

Deep dive into Kenya’s revenue, spending, and fiscal priorities under the 2024/25 budget

Budget Context & Objectives

The 2024/2025 National Budget of Kenya, originally estimated at ~Ksh 3.99 trillion, was later revised to about Ksh 3.87 trillion via the first Supplementary Budget. :contentReference[oaicite:0]{index=0} This budget reflects the government’s commitment to fiscal consolidation, reducing non-essential spending, and prioritizing key sectors under its Bottom-up Economic Transformation Agenda (BETA). :contentReference[oaicite:1]{index=1}

Key Fiscal Figures

  • Revised Total Budget: ~Ksh 3.87 trillion after adjustment. :contentReference[oaicite:2]{index=2}
  • Original Total Expenditure Proposal: Ksh 3.99 trillion. :contentReference[oaicite:3]{index=3}
  • Projected Ordinary Revenue: ~Ksh 2.95 trillion. :contentReference[oaicite:4]{index=4}
  • Deficit: Increased to ~3.6% of GDP after downward revision. :contentReference[oaicite:5]{index=5}
  • County Equitable Share: Ksh 400.1 billion. :contentReference[oaicite:6]{index=6}

Revenue Strategy

To fund the budget, the government relies on a mix of revenue sources:

  • Tax Revenue: A large portion comes from ordinary revenue — including PAYE, VAT, and corporate tax. :contentReference[oaicite:14]{index=14}
  • Domestic Borrowing: A major financing route, with significant plans for treasury bonds and internal loans. :contentReference[oaicite:15]{index=15}
  • External Borrowing: Sourced through loans from international partners to finance deficit. :contentReference[oaicite:16]{index=16}
  • Grants: Modest component, used to support priority projects and social services. :contentReference[oaicite:17]{index=17}

Priority Programs & Policy Focus (2024/25)

Under the Bottom-up Economic Transformation Agenda (BETA), the government emphasizes inclusive growth and equity. :contentReference[oaicite:18]{index=18}

  • Agricultural Transformation & Food Security: Boosting productivity through subsidies, irrigation, and youth-led value chains.
  • Affordable Housing: Increased allocation for housing delivery to reduce the national housing gap.
  • Universal Health Coverage (UHC): Expanding access to healthcare for vulnerable populations.
  • Digital Economy / “Digital Superhighway”: Scaling ICT infrastructure and connectivity.
  • MSME Support & Job Creation: Stimulating micro- and small business development under the national transformation agenda.
  • Anti-corruption and Governance: Boosting oversight institutions like EACC, Judiciary, and Parliament. :contentReference[oaicite:19]{index=19}
  • Fiscal Consolidation: Austerity measures and spending rationalization following public outcry and revised budget. :contentReference[oaicite:20]{index=20}

Risks & Challenges

While this budget charts a strategic path, there are several risks:

  • Debt Burden: Nearly half of the budget goes toward debt servicing, constraining fiscal flexibility. :contentReference[oaicite:21]{index=21}
  • Deficit Financing Risk: Heavy reliance on borrowing could exacerbate vulnerability, especially under weaker economic growth.
  • Revenue Shortfalls: Earlier tax hike proposals were rolled back due to public protests, prompting downward revision of expected revenue. :contentReference[oaicite:22]{index=22}
  • Absorption Capacity: The Controller of Budget flagged issues with low absorption and pending bills (~Ksh 524.8B). :contentReference[oaicite:23]{index=23}
