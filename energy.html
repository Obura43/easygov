Energy & Electricity Access

Powering Kenya’s transformation through reliable, affordable, and clean energy solutions.

Home/ National Projects/ Energy & Electricity

Overview

Kenya’s energy sector has experienced a transformative surge, with electricity access rising from 25% in 2013 to over 75% as of 2025, thanks to government investments exceeding KSh 120 billion. With an installed capacity of 3,074 MW, over 90% of power generation comes from renewable sources, including geothermal, wind, solar, and hydroelectricity.

Key Achievements

  • Electricity Access: 75% of the population (approx. 38 million people)
  • Installed Capacity: 3,074 MW, with an additional 500 MW under development
  • Renewable Energy: 90% of electricity generated from clean sources
  • Last Mile Connectivity: Over 3 million households connected in rural and underserved areas
  • Energy Investments: Over KSh 120 billion in infrastructure and expansion projects
Geothermal

Olkaria Geothermal Power Complex

Kenya's flagship geothermal site located in Nakuru County, generating 865 MW — approximately 40% of the national electricity supply. This positions Kenya as the largest geothermal energy producer in Africa.

Operational
Connectivity

Last Mile Connectivity Program

Nationwide initiative connecting over 3 million households in rural counties such as Turkana, Marsabit, and Kilifi, reducing energy poverty and enabling socio-economic development.

Ongoing
Wind

Lake Turkana Wind Power Project

Africa’s largest wind farm located in Marsabit County, with a capacity of 310 MW. It contributes around 17% of Kenya's total installed capacity and provides clean energy to over 1 million homes.

Operational
Solar

Garissa Solar Power Plant

East Africa’s first large-scale grid-connected solar plant located in Garissa County, with a capacity of 54.6 MW, significantly boosting clean energy generation in the region.

Operational

Impact

  • Improved quality of life with reliable 24/7 electricity access in urban and rural areas
  • Enabled over 500,000 businesses to increase efficiency and expand operations
  • Significantly reduced Kenya’s carbon footprint by shifting to renewable energy sources
  • Created more than 15,000 direct and indirect jobs in energy generation and distribution sectors
  • Enhanced education and healthcare services through powered schools and medical facilities
  • Strengthened digital economy by powering internet infrastructure and technology hubs
